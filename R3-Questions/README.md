# R3 Mathematical Problem-Solving Bank

R3 is a difficult, proof-oriented mathematical reasoning assessment. It is intended to reveal abstraction, persistence, pattern recognition, and rigorous reasoning under unfamiliar conditions. It is not an engineering-knowledge test, and it should not be described as an IQ test unless it has been professionally validated as one.

## Recommended live format

- **Time:** 60 minutes
- **Form:** five questions selected from this bank
- **Score:** 20 points per question, 100 total
- **Expected experience:** most candidates will not finish most questions
- **Scoring:** award substantial partial credit for a useful lemma, correct reduction, or rigorous progress
- **Tools:** no internet, AI, symbolic algebra, or external reference material; provide scratch paper and any formulas that are not intended knowledge
- **Knowledge floor:** algebra, functions, elementary probability, basic calculus, and elementary proof; use the linear-algebra item only for roles where that background is declared

The boss's proposed “average around 20” should be treated as a **pilot calibration target**, not a quota or proof of quality. A form is useful only if scores distinguish levels of reasoning. If almost everyone receives zero, or if success mainly tracks prior olympiad training, change the form.

## Source families

The questions in this bank are original. Their style and scoring approach are informed by these public sources:

1. **William Lowell Putnam Mathematical Competition** — the closest match to an extremely difficult undergraduate problem-solving exam. The MAA publishes [past problems and solutions](https://maa.org/maa-putnam-archive/). Its [2024 results](https://maa.org/news/results-of-the-85th-william-lowell-putnam-mathematical-competition/) report an average near 8/120 and median 2, and the MAA notes in its [discussion of the competition's difficulty](https://maa.org/math-values/the-putnam-exam-why-do-we-compete/) that zero medians have historically been common. This is useful as an upper difficulty reference, not as a hiring test to copy literally.
2. **STEP Mathematics** — [past papers, solutions, and mark schemes](https://www.ocr.org.uk/students/step-mathematics/preparing-for-step/) are published by OCR. Each question is worth 20 points, partial progress is rewarded, and questions emphasize applying mathematics in novel ways. This is the best model for the R3 scoring rubric.
3. **Oxford Mathematics Admissions Test archive** — Oxford publishes [past MAT papers, solutions, feedback, and score statistics](https://www.maths.ox.ac.uk/study-here/undergraduate-study/maths-admissions-test). MAT was designed to test depth rather than breadth and to remain approachable without Further Mathematics. It is a useful model for the accessible end of R3. Oxford replaced MAT with TMUA beginning in 2026, but the archive remains valuable.
4. **International Mathematics Competition for University Students** — the official [problem, solution, and marking-scheme archive](https://www.imc-math.org.uk/?item=info&item=problems) offers a broad range of proof problems in algebra, analysis, combinatorics, and number theory.

## Important hiring constraint

Difficulty alone does not validate an employment test. Mathematical competition experience is trainable and unequally distributed. Before R3 becomes a gate:

- identify the reasoning construct the company intends to measure;
- pilot every form on strong employees and applicants outside mathematics-heavy backgrounds;
- measure whether R3 adds predictive value beyond R0, R1, R2, and R4;
- provide reasonable accommodations;
- monitor subgroup selection rates and candidate reactions; and
- remove items that primarily reward recalled contest techniques.

The U.S. Office of Personnel Management notes both the predictive value and the subgroup differences associated with [cognitive-ability tests](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/assessment-and-selection/other-assessment-methods/cognitive-ability-tests/). The EEOC's guidance on [employment tests and selection procedures](https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/employment-tests-and-selection-procedures) emphasizes that procedures with adverse impact need job-related justification and consideration of less discriminatory alternatives. For those reasons, R3 should initially be a separately reported experimental score or a modest gate, not the dominant hiring signal.

## Form assembly

A balanced five-question form should normally contain:

- one accessible question such as M09;
- one medium number-theory or invariant question such as M01 or M11;
- one combinatorics/probability question such as M02, M05, or M10;
- one analysis/algebra question such as M04, M07, M08, or M12; and
- one genuinely difficult discriminator such as M03.

Do not place five “very hard” questions on the same form. A target mean near 20/100 usually requires an entry point, partial-credit opportunities, and one or two questions that strong candidates can substantially solve.
