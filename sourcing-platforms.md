# Sourcing Channels and Job-Posting Strategy

> [!CAUTION]
> **⚖️ This document is not legal advice. Before posting any role, legal counsel must confirm the following:**
> - **Job ad wording**: whether to describe the role as a contractor or an EOR position, and that it is consistent with how our current Philippines staff are engaged.
> - **Pay transparency**: the EU Pay Transparency Directive is already in force in Poland, and Romania and Bulgaria are still legislating.
> - **Contests and prizes**: running a contest and paying out prizes brings country-specific rules on prize promotions, prizes and tax, plus OFAC sanctions screening for every winner.
> - **Candidate data**: collecting and storing it must comply with GDPR, India's DPDP Act, the Philippines Data Privacy Act and similar laws.

> Research date: 2026-10-15
> Companion documents: [country-hiring-research.md](country-hiring-research.md) (country policy) and [process.md](process.md) (hiring process).
> Most prices come from third-party or dated sources. **Get written quotes from each platform before committing budget.**

## Core strategy: source through contests, not only job ads

Our process (R0–R4) is **open to anyone, very selective, and structured like a contest**. The most effective way to fill it is to go where strong algorithmic engineers already compete, not only to post on general job boards.

We recommend three tiers:

| Tier | Goal | Channels | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Contests** | **Precision**: strong candidates choose to enter | Sponsored Codeforces rounds, our own public contest, olympiad and ICPC communities | Smaller volume, highest quality |
| **2. Mass platforms** | **Reach**: maximum exposure | LinkedIn, Naukri, JobStreet, Indeed | High but noisy volume; R0 does the filtering |
| **3. Local IT boards** | **Local depth**: engineers actively job-hunting in each country | Just Join IT, DEV.BG, Djinni, DOU, Cutshort, etc. | Balance of quality and volume |

**Reference model: Telegram (contest.com)**
- Telegram runs public contests every 1–2 months, with more than $2.5M in prizes awarded so far.
- Telegram says winning a contest is the only way to join the team, and some winners are offered paid projects first.
- Our R4 work sample could eventually become a **public contest with prizes** that also works as a sourcing tool.

## 1. Global and cross-border channels (ranked)

| # | Channel | Rough cost | Reach and fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Sponsor a Codeforces round** (Div 2 or Div 1+2), with our hiring link in the round announcement | Sponsorship fee by quote (not published), plus about $3–10k in prizes | 15–25k registrants per round (think-cell's first round drew over 22.5k). Best self-selection. Reaches India, Poland, Romania and Ukraine |
| **2** | **Run our own public prize contest** (the Telegram model), promoted on Codeforces, Hacker News and Reddit | $5–20k prize pool | Turns the work sample into a sourcing tool, with the highest-quality signal |
| **3** | **Promoted LinkedIn job**, geo-targeted to PL, RO, BG, UA, IN and PH | About $300–1,000 per role per month (pay per click) | Largest raw reach. R0 does the filtering |
| **4** | **Hacker News "Who is Hiring"** monthly thread, plus Wellfound and Himalayas | Free | Strong engineers who choose to apply. Low volume, high quality |
| **5** | **Sponsored LeetCode, HackerEarth or CodeChef contest** (India-focused) | Quote only | Very large Indian pool, but noisier than Codeforces |
| **6** | **We Work Remotely** or **Remotive** (pick one) | $299 for 30 days | Global remote job seekers. Moderate algorithmic fit |

**Notes**
- **Codeforces activity by country** (from a single undated blog dataset): India has about 10k active rated users a month, Romania about 120 and Poland about 80. Eastern European users have higher average ratings.
- **Country-specific contest template:** JetBrains has run a "Coding Cup Romania" on Codeforces.
- **Not recommended:** talent marketplaces such as Toptal, Turing, Andela and Arc. They replace our vetting with theirs and are expensive (for example a 20% fee or a buyout to convert a hire), which does not fit an open-entry process. Use them only as a fallback for one urgent contractor.
- **Stack Overflow Jobs** shut down in 2022. The 2024 relaunch covers only the US, Germany, France, the UK and the Netherlands, so it doesn't reach our target countries.

**Mix:** LinkedIn + Naukri (with strict screening) + Cutshort/Instahyre + Wellfound (free), with a separate contest budget

**Local norms**
- Notice periods are 30–90 days, so always ask for the notice period or last working day as a screening question.
- State the salary range as an annual figure in LPA (lakhs per annum). Without it, application volume and salary expectations get out of hand.
- Label the engagement clearly, for example "Remote – Independent Contractor (USD)" or "via EOR". Candidates often ask about provident fund (PF) and gratuity.

## 3. Job-ad principles for all countries
1. **Write in English.** IT ads in every target country accept English. Georgia works better with a bilingual English/Georgian ad, and Romania and Bulgaria benefit from a short local-language summary.
2. **Show a salary range** as gross monthly pay in USD or EUR. The good IT boards treat this as normal and it clearly raises response rates. No Fluff Jobs requires it, and Djinni effectively does.
3. **State the contract type clearly**, for example "B2B / Contractor, fully remote" or "Full-time via EOR". **Legal must confirm the wording.**
4. **Link straight to our public test or contest**, and stress that entry is open and background doesn't matter. That in itself attracts strong candidates.
5. **In India, add screening questions** on notice period and expected salary.
6. **Explain our AI policy and the time each stage takes**, in line with the Entry principles in [process.md](process.md).

## 5. Measuring results
Following the Quality control section of [process.md](process.md), track for every channel:
- **Applications** and **pass rate at each stage** (R0 → R1 → R2 → R3 → R4 → offer)
- **Cost per candidate who passes R2**, which says more than cost per application
- **Average scores by country and channel**

After the first cohort, drop the weak channels and move budget to the ones with the best pass rates.
