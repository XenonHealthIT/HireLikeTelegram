# Sourcing Channels and Job-Posting Strategy

> [!CAUTION]
> **⚖️ This document is not legal advice. Before posting any role, legal counsel must confirm the following:**
> - **Job ad wording**: every role is an independent-contractor engagement. The ad must not describe it as employment, for example by promising salary, benefits, or fixed working hours.
> - **Pay transparency**: Romania and Bulgaria are still writing the EU Pay Transparency Directive into national law. It covers employers, so check whether it applies to contractor roles.
> - **Contests and prizes**: running a contest and paying out prizes brings country-specific rules on prize promotions, prizes and tax, plus OFAC sanctions screening for every winner.
> - **Candidate data**: collecting and storing it must comply with GDPR and India's DPDP Act.

> Research date: 2026-10-15 (updated 2026-10-19)
> Target countries: **Bulgaria, India, Romania** (independent contractors only). See [country-hiring-research.md](country-hiring-research.md) for country policy and [process.md](process.md) for the hiring process.
> Most prices come from third-party or dated sources. **Get written quotes from each platform before committing budget.**

## Core strategy: source through contests, not only job ads

Our process (R0–R4) is **open to anyone, very selective, and structured like a contest**. The most effective way to fill it is to go where strong algorithmic engineers already compete, not only to post on general job boards.

We recommend three tiers:

| Tier | Goal | Channels | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Contests** | **Precision**: strong candidates choose to enter | Sponsored Codeforces rounds, our own public contest, olympiad and ICPC communities | Smaller volume, highest quality |
| **2. Mass platforms** | **Reach**: maximum exposure | LinkedIn, Naukri | High but noisy volume; R0 does the filtering |
| **3. Local IT boards** | **Local depth**: engineers actively job-hunting in each country | DEV.BG, Cutshort/Instahyre, eJobs | Balance of quality and volume |

**Reference model: Telegram (contest.com)**
- Telegram runs public contests every 1–2 months, with more than $2.5M in prizes awarded so far.
- Telegram says winning a contest is the only way to join the team, and some winners are offered paid projects first.
- Our R4 work sample could eventually become a **public contest with prizes** that also works as a sourcing tool.

- **Codeforces activity by country** (from a single undated blog dataset): India has about 10k active rated users a month and Romania about 120. Eastern European users have higher average ratings.

- **Not recommended:** talent marketplaces such as Toptal, Turing, Andela and Arc. They replace our vetting with theirs and are expensive (for example a 20% fee or a buyout to convert a hire), which does not fit an open-entry process.

**Local norms**
- Many candidates are serving 30–90 day notice periods, so ask for their availability date as a screening question.
- State the annual rate range in USD or LPA (lakhs per annum). Without it, application volume and expectations get out of hand.
- Label the role "Remote – Independent Contractor (USD)". Candidates often ask about provident fund (PF) and gratuity, so make clear that these do not apply.

## 3. Job-ad principles
1. **Write in English.** IT ads in all three countries accept English. Romania and Bulgaria benefit from a short local-language summary.
2. **Show a rate range**, as monthly or hourly pay in USD or EUR. The good IT boards treat this as normal, and it clearly raises response rates.
3. **State the engagement clearly:** "Independent Contractor, fully remote". The contractor invoices us and is responsible for their own local tax and labor reporting. **Legal must confirm the wording.**
4. **Link straight to our public test or contest**, and stress that entry is open and background doesn't matter. That in itself attracts strong candidates.
5. **In India, add screening questions** on availability date and expected rate.
6. **Explain our AI policy and the time each stage takes**, in line with the Entry principles in [process.md](process.md).

## 5. Measuring results
Following the Quality control section of [process.md](process.md), track for every channel:
- **Applications** and the **pass rate at each stage** (R0 → R1 → R2 → R3 → R4 → offer)
- **Cost per candidate who passes R2**, which says more than cost per application
- **Average scores by country and channel**

After the first cohort, drop the weak channels and move budget to the ones with the best pass rates.
